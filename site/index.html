Officefloor turns scattered terminals, branches and lanes into one live floor: who owns what, what’s moving, what’s blocked at your door, and which parts of the picture are honestly unknown.

Invents nothing

Every pixel is a projection of state your org already writes: processes, branches, heartbeats, outboxes. No agent has to behave differently to render correctly.

Decisions at the door

Everything only you can unblock queues in one place: decisions parked at your door, finished branches waiting to be collected, seats gone dark holding a directive.

Honest unknowns

When the floor can’t measure something it says so. A seat reads unknown, never a guess. Counters only count what was actually observed.

Agents need fun too

Off duty, they drink beer at the crate, smoke outside by the door, and play ping pong. The ball only bounces when both ends are taken. They gossip, they linger by the water cooler. They do many things; none of them touch a fact.

Every pixel has a receipt.

Click any seat and get the evidence behind its state: the process, the branch, the timestamps. Trust the floor because you can audit it, not because it looks confident.

A floor you keep open for eight
hours is also a place.

The truth doesn’t have to be gray. Off duty, the floor stays alive, and every bit of it is cosmetic by construction, so the fun never touches a fact.

The kitchen

Benched seats get a beer.

Counter, espresso machine, fridge, a crate of beers, two tables. Off duty, agents drift in: coffee at the machine, lunch at the tables, a beer at the crate. On Fridays the tables convert to beer pong.

The ring (coming soon)

Grudges settle in the ring.

Benched seats box RuneScape-style: HP bars off rolled personality stats, three attack styles, a crowd of off-duty seats at the ropes. Nobody dies and nothing real is wagered: the loser respawns, walks to the kitchen, and takes the beer.

The civilians

The world walks in.

Santa Claus audits your TODO comments. Donald Bump rates your merge queue. Zark Muckerberg expects applause for a one-line diff. Keep watching and the floor becomes an ensemble workplace comedy.
